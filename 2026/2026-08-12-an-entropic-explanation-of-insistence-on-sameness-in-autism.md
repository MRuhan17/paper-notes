# 2026-08-12-an-entropic-explanation-of-insistence-on-sameness-in-autism.md

**Status:** Read

## Summary

This paper proposes an **information-theoretic framework** to explain insistence on sameness in autism as a behavior aimed at **reducing surprise and uncertainty**. The author models the environment as a random variable \(R\) and an individual's memory as \(M\), using the entropy distance \(D_H(R,M)=H(R|M)+H(M|R)\). The framework suggests that uncertainty can be reduced either by learning more about the environment or by restricting the environment so that it matches what is already stored in memory. The paper focuses on the latter as a possible explanation for insistence on sameness. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}

The proposed cognitive model assumes that stimuli are processed through **perception, classification, memory, and prediction**, with nearest-neighbor classification and persistent memory. Surprise during perception and uncertainty during prediction are treated as quantities that can exceed thresholds for **sensory overload or anxiety**. Known and deterministic sequences produce low entropy, while unfamiliar stimuli and multiple possible future events increase uncertainty.

Using this framework, the paper connects behaviors such as **pedantry, repetitive and ritualistic behavior, rigidity, vigilance, and aversion to learning** to attempts to control or minimize uncertainty. It also proposes that self-stimulation activities may help maintain a low-surprise "comfort zone," although this is explicitly presented as a conjecture.

A practical contribution is a set of **therapeutic design guidelines**. The proposed approach emphasizes tangible and realistically simulated environments, learning relationships between representations and tangible objects separately, preserving contextual information, avoiding unexpected shortcuts in learned sequences, and carefully controlling branching or multiple-choice situations. Learning therapy is formulated as an optimization problem that maximizes the mutual information between the environment and memory while keeping surprise and uncertainty below specified thresholds. The authors suggest that this formulation could eventually support algorithmic therapies or robotic live-in caregivers. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

For validation, the paper proposes both direct experiments with individuals and a **Turing-test-like approach using simulated "digital twins"** in virtual environments. The latter is suggested as a preliminary way to test whether the framework produces behavior that therapists recognize as consistent with the target phenomena.

Overall, the paper is primarily a **theoretical and computational hypothesis**, rather than an experimentally validated account of autism. Its central claim is that insistence on sameness may be understood as a special case of a broader uncertainty-minimization behavior, with the distinctive manifestation attributed to the cognitive limitations assumed by the model. The proposed autism definition and therapeutic implications remain conjectural and require validation. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}
